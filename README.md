# FocusFix for macOS

FocusFix is a personal macOS app for recording computer activity, marking work time, taking breaks, and reviewing sessions. It is under development and is separate from the developer’s N-of-1 study. It is not a medical device or a validated measure of productivity or fatigue.

## Getting started

1. Open FocusFix and choose whether to enable recording. Grant macOS Input Monitoring access if you want activity capture.
2. Select **Start work** for a manual session, or choose **Task** to open projects and use **Work on this**, or choose **Work target** and confirm **Start work**.
3. Use **Take a break** and **I’m back** for explicit breaks. **End work** saves a manual session.
4. Open **Sessions** to review, export, or delete local recordings. Days with multiple recordings appear as expandable folders. Exports combine selected recordings into a few narrow CSV tables per day, with shared session lookups.
5. Open **Quick tour** for a nine-step practice flow. The tour uses demo data and changes no settings or recordings.

Automatic recording is optional in **Settings → Privacy & Data Sharing** and has its own acknowledgement. It may include non-work computer use, begins with an **Unknown** label, and continues while the menu-bar app runs. **Pause recording** or **Quit** stops capture. Closing a window alone may leave it running. The visible status shows whether recording is active. Five minutes of continuous idle time, sleep/lock, or loss of Input Monitoring permission also stop capture.

FocusFix records timing and counts for generic keyboard activity, delete-key use, clicks, scrolling, app switches, and one-second mouse movement summaries. It does not save typed words, individual letter keys, app names, window titles, URLs, screenshots, or exact cursor paths. Detailed timing can still be sensitive. See the [privacy page](https://krisjanci.github.io/FocusFix-Website/privacy.html).

## Storage and daily activity exports

Completed recordings, including existing saved recordings, are compacted in the background using lossless ZIP compression. Each archive is verified against the original files before their uncompressed copies are removed. The app keeps a small storage index for fast history listing. Recordings that are unfinished, unreadable, or would become larger stay in their existing format. Compression preserves the values already recorded. New schema 12 recordings use the reduced movement precision described below; existing recordings are not rewritten to that schema.

In **Sessions**, choose **Export**, **Export Selected**, or **Export All**. Each date gets a folder containing only tables with records: `inputs.csv`, `movement.csv`, `breaks.csv`, `checkins.csv` and `changes.csv`. Multiple recordings on the same day share these files. The entire export uses the Mac’s timezone captured at export start. Exports are plain folders generated on demand.

Open `inputs.csv` for keyboard/click/scroll events, `movement.csv` for mouse measurements, `breaks.csv` for break actions and timing, `checkins.csv` for answers, and `changes.csv` for context/task changes, reminder requests and check-in actions. Columns use units such as `elapsed_s`, `hold_s` and `distance_points`. Elapsed values stay on the original logical recording clock. A small `session` reference joins each row to the shared `sessions.csv`, which holds original IDs, start anchors, elapsed origins and durable bounds.

`metadata.json` contains identity/protocol lookups, source field mappings, remaining settings, revisions and low-volume log details. Recorded fields are stored in one place; the export has no per-input JSON copies or wide union of unrelated columns. Movement start/end seconds are rounded to whole numbers, distance points to the nearest 0.1 and since-movement seconds to at most two decimals, using nearest rounding with ties away from zero. Other numeric trailing zeros are removed without rounding, and quoting is used only when needed. Blank answers and unavailable measurements remain blank; zero remains zero. Each movement interval stays in one row even when its rounded start and end are equal. Other intervals retain their original bounds. Historical exports use original timestamps for ordering, daily grouping and cross-day references before rounding. The metadata supports reconstructing retained values, including historical survey fields and precise JSON timestamps; discarded movement decimals are not copied into metadata.

Inputs and movement omit `phase` and `since_break_s`. Inputs retain `repeat` (automatic key repeats), `hold_s` (measured per-key duration without saving key identities) and `precise` (fine-grained scroll deltas). Future internal recordings use these same reductions. Exact observed coverage is saved separately as merged intervals in `observed_coverage.json`, before each durable checkpoint advances, so reopening a recording preserves progress totals and gaps. That sidecar appears once in export metadata. Older recordings remain readable using their original movement coverage. Context intent remains in `changes.csv`. Uncheckpointed tails remain present but must not receive work credit; use exact coverage and durable bounds in `sessions.csv` for calculations. Exporting does not rewrite recordings or previous exports.

The start-of-day survey has been removed. On launch, FocusFix deletes its old local response file and preferences, retrying after failures. Previously exported copies remain separate. Timed fatigue/productivity check-ins remain available.

## Personal survey check-ins

Open **Settings → Privacy & Data Sharing** and turn on **Survey check-ins**. The **Prompts per day** slider selects 1–5 (default 2). Surveys start off; the frequency controls appear only when check-ins are enabled. Each optional check-in asks for mental fatigue and self-reported productivity using two sliders.

FocusFix saves random opportunities across the remainder of each local calendar day. It requests up to your chosen number while recording and outside breaks, with at least 30 minutes between new check-ins, including across midnight and app restarts. Missed times coalesce into one opportunity rather than a burst. Days with limited recording time may have fewer prompts. Requested prompts count even if skipped or unanswered. Changing the count preserves requests already made that day; turning surveys off immediately clears pending and open check-ins. Settings and today's schedule survive relaunches.

Personal check-ins do not enroll you in a study or enable sharing. Existing recording and sharing choices still apply. The separate reviewed-study configuration remains gated.

## Accounts and optional sharing

Guest use keeps recordings on the Mac. An optional account supports a saved profile and private groups. Signing in never opts you into recording sharing.

For product development, the developer can manually approve a signed-in account and enable collection in Supabase. The user must then separately review the sharing details and opt in. Only completed recording segments started after consent may upload. A user can turn sharing off and request deletion of hosted recordings in **Settings → Privacy & Data Sharing**. Local files, exports, and backups are separate. The older research uploader remains disabled.

The server configuration starts with collection **off**, no approved accounts, a private bucket, and a default limit of ten archives per approved account. The desktop app contains a publishable Supabase client key, never a service-role key. See [development sharing operations](supabase/DEVELOPMENT-SHARING.md) before enabling the service.

## Build and verification

Open `FocusFix/FocusFix.xcodeproj` in Xcode on macOS 14 or later. Run `bash scripts/test.sh` for local tests and `npm test` plus `npm run test:database` in the `supabase` directory for service tests. Release packaging and content checks are in `scripts/verify-release.sh` and `scripts/package-website.sh`.

A public download is not posted yet. A release still needs the database migration and deletion function deployed, a disposable-project upload/deletion check, a signed and notarized package, a fresh-Mac run, and final App Store privacy answers. A passing source build alone does not complete those checks.

Completed Saturday and Sunday goals add a day to work and healthy-habit streaks. Missed weekends stay optional and use no freezes. Group scores require the `202611150002_count_qualifying_weekends.sql` migration; saved qualifying weekend history counts on recalculation.

Group work streaks sync observed declared-work intervals with retry-safe replacements, including historical corrections. Positive, unfrozen streaks have a gentle burning animation that respects Reduce Motion. Account buttons use FocusFix teal. Group rows show the same shared thumbnail to you and other members. Newly added account photos default to sharing with group members. Change **Show my profile photo to members of my groups** in **Settings → Privacy & Data Sharing**; changes to existing photos save immediately. You can also turn sharing off before adding a photo, and that account-specific choice is remembered on this Mac. Existing photos retain their saved visibility when replaced. Photo visibility and survey controls live in Privacy & Data Sharing; the profile window links to Settings. Imports prepare the private photo and bounded 64-pixel thumbnail together, so failed imports cannot leave mismatched images. Group photos update on the next group refresh.

Groups include **Work streak**, **Healthy habits**, and **Chat** tabs in both the side panel and the Groups window. Chat displays **Coming soon** only: it adds no requests, subscriptions, database tables, or message storage.

Groups and Analytics share one right-side panel. The bottom-right **Analytics** button shows blurred illustrative charts and **Coming soon**; switching panels keeps the same window width. Appearance is an icon-only button beside the recording status; each click cycles System → Light → Dark → System, with no menu.

The full-width **Badges** button at the bottom of Analytics opens a collection with one large badge per row. Earn permanent work-streak badges at 5, 10, 20, 30, 50, 75, and 100 completed days. Each day requires 60 minutes of declared work; completed weekends count, and the existing weekend and freeze rules protect continuity. **Joined FocusFix** unlocks after account sign-up/sign-in and stays locked for guests. Work achievements follow the local progress card and do not sync across devices. Existing qualifying history is backfilled without a burst of old celebrations or alerts.

New awards show a full-window badge celebration over a blurred background and send **You've gained a badge!** through macOS when notifications are allowed. Click to dismiss: the badge shrinks along an arc toward Analytics; Reduce Motion uses a fade. Red circles on Analytics and its Badges entry stay until the collection is opened. Earned badge details and celebrations include **Share to LinkedIn** with its logo; clicking prepares a 1200 × 1200 badge PNG and opens the default browser to LinkedIn’s post composer with a suggested one-line caption. Attach the image manually before publishing. The sharing panel offers **Show badge image**, **Copy caption**, and **Open LinkedIn**; use Copy caption if LinkedIn ignores the browser prefill. No post is published automatically. Click elsewhere or press Escape to dismiss details. Future badges remain visible in grey with their unlock requirements.

Activity labels now follow **Start work** and **End work**, including timer-card setup and menu-bar equivalents. Automatic recording outside declared work is **Unknown**. Manual context menus and history corrections are no longer offered. Breaks remain separate from activity context. Narrow daily exports retain original historical `non_work` values for lossless reconstruction; current controls still offer Work and Unknown. Hosted uploads retain their existing formats and allowlists.

The timer card offers **Task** (a document icon) and **Conditions**. Task replaces the main content with projects and checklists inside the same window, without changing its dimensions or the Groups/Analytics panel. The profile, app logo/title, recording-status statement, streak, appearance control, and large work buttons are hidden to give the checklist more space. One bordered back arrow, matching Badges, sits beside Projects or the current project name; it returns one level at a time. Projects are optional: the normal timer’s Start work records without a project when none is selected; there is no separate no-project action in the projects list. Create a project with a custom color, add and reorder tasks, then use a task’s small rounded teal play button (**Work on this**) or the project’s **Untasked Work** action. These actions start work when idle or switch assignments during work without resetting the timer or work target, then return straight to the main timer after success. Permission prompts or save failures keep the task page available for attention. Browsing and editing never start recording.

Projects initially show **8 rows**, with a centered grey **+** beneath the list instead of in the header. Taller rows adjust to fill the available canvas at that limit. The add button is three times as wide as the arrow. When more projects remain, the slightly lighter centered down-arrow **expands the list by eight more rows**, keeping all earlier entries. Expanded lists scroll; the up-arrow collapses back to the initial eight.

Inside a project, **Untasked Work** stays separate from the initial **7 task rows**; completed tasks count toward that limit. Task rows also expand to fill the canvas. The down-arrow reveals seven more tasks without replacing existing rows, and the up-arrow collapses the list. The project-creation button is absent here. A matching wide grey **+** below the tasks reveals the text field and small save **+** only when requested. Enter or the small **+** saves, collapses the entry, expands the list as needed, and brings the new task into view. Escape cancels the draft. Task titles use slightly larger 15-point text above indented Worked and Predicted subtitles. Compact single-line task titles expose their full text on hover.

Every catalog includes **Random Tasks**, a default project whose name is fixed and which cannot be deleted; its color and tasks remain editable. Existing catalogs gain this folder without changing other IDs or tasks. An existing Random Tasks folder is adopted in place. Projects move to the top when you explicitly start or switch work on them, not when opened, renamed, or checked off. Drag projects to arrange them manually; accessible **Move up / Move down** actions are also available. A teal insertion line shows whether the row will be placed above or below its target before release. Manual order survives relaunch and stays in effect until another work action promotes a project.

Projects and project headings use a 23-point rounded semibold title, just below the main FocusFix title’s 25 points. **Edit** switches the page into editing mode: red **×** controls replace task play buttons and appear beside removable projects. Click a name to rename it inline, then press Enter, choose the checkmark, or select **Done** to save. Invalid names keep the editor open with an error. Escape cancels the name draft or leaves editing mode. Project colors use the former tag palette: choose a preset swatch or the last blank **+** swatch to open the native custom-color picker. New projects show the palette inline; in Edit, click the project’s colored circle to open it. A checkmark identifies the selected preset, and custom colors remain saved with the project. Project color controls are available in Edit; Random Tasks keeps its protected name and lock. Project deletion asks for confirmation; task deletion removes the task from its checklist while preserving recorded history. No three-dot menus remain on project/task rows.

While working on a task, the timer also offers a green outlined **Finished task** chip on the same row as the project and task name, below the general timer controls. It checks off the active task, ends project/task timing, and continues declared work without a project, preserving the running timer and target credit. The completed task saves its recorded total with the task and project names. It is hidden during breaks and when no task is active.

Checking a task shows a green tick immediately. Three seconds later it shrinks to 85%, becomes muted, and slides to the completed rows at the bottom. Unchecking restores its canonical checklist position; drag ordering or accessible Move up/Move down actions update that order. Teal placement guides appear for both unfinished and completed task rows. Tasks move within their current section, so dragging never checks or unchecks a task. Completed tasks can be manually reordered; the next newly completed task still appends to the end. Checking off the active task in the checklist continues as Untasked Work in the same project. Pressing **Finished task** beside the assignment in the timer instead ends project/task timing and continues the overall declared-work session with no project. It preserves the running timer and target, saves the task/project names and completion date, and totals the task’s observed work across saved recordings up to completion. Breaks, check-ins, Unknown activity and unobserved gaps are excluded. The saved total appears beneath the completed task; hover to see its frozen task/project names. Interrupted total saves retry on launch; reopening protects the task from stale background results. Unchecking does not resume it automatically. Reduce Motion uses a brief fade. Completed tasks remain visible and editable.

Project colors use the timer’s existing background tint. Each assignment change preserves earlier time; breaks and Unknown activity are excluded. New work periods begin without a project, while breaks retain the assignment. Renaming or deleting projects/tasks preserves historical name/color snapshots. Deleting the active task switches to Untasked Work; deleting its project switches to no project.

Projects and tasks save privately on this Mac in `projects.json`. Recording assignment history lives separately in `work_assignments.json`, appears in Sessions, and is included in local exports. Neither file is included in shared recording uploads. Older tags remain readable in session history and exports; new work uses projects instead. Click a task title outside Edit mode to open its details sheet. An optional multiline description and a 0.5–3 Focus Sessions estimate save automatically; estimates use half-session steps and default to 1 FS. One Focus Session means one hour of focused work. Done or Escape flushes pending edits; save failures keep the draft open for retry. Descriptions and estimates stay in the private local catalog and are not added to recording exports or uploads. There are no due dates, priorities, subtasks, or account sync in this version.

Project rows show indented Total work time; task rows and details show Worked time in hours and minutes. Totals use retained observed recording history plus live observed work, excluding breaks, check-ins, Unknown activity and unavailable gaps. Project totals include Untasked Work and historical work on deleted tasks. Removing recordings removes their contribution; frozen completed-task summaries remain preserved. History loads in the background and unreadable history shows Unavailable with Refresh instead of a misleading zero.

Project and task rows use a neutral pressed background that darkens in light mode and lightens in dark mode, including while held for reordering. Opening a project applies its subtle timer-style tint across the full main content area, including the Groups and Analytics footer buttons, and the task details sheet. The expanded side panel remains untinted. Leaving projects restores the main timer’s existing appearance. Double-click a title in the task details sheet to rename it inline, with no extra edit buttons: Enter or leaving the field saves, Escape cancels the title draft, and Done saves before closing. Task details stay centered over the original 490-point main area even when Groups or Analytics extends the window.

When idle, confirming **Work target** with **Start work** begins work, including when automatic capture is Unknown. During work, **Done** changes the target without restarting or losing credit already earned. Cancel leaves recording unchanged.

Unanswered personal check-ins repeat their notification every 10 minutes while recording and outside breaks, until answered or explicitly skipped. Repeats belong to the same check-in and do not use another daily slot; the 30-minute minimum applies to new check-ins. The same question and **Answer**/**Skip** actions appear directly below **Start work**/**End work**. Opening the survey pauses repeats; closing it without answering leaves it pending and starts another ten-minute wait. Breaks defer the pending reminder. Stopping recording or turning check-ins off cancels it.

The timer’s **+Conditions** button opens a quick Conditions window. Choose one reusable label per category: Location, Music, and Work setting come with starter choices. Every category is optional. Add your own categories and labels, or use **Edit** to rename/remove catalog entries. Catalog edits save independently; Cancel discards only the session selection. **Start work** confirms conditions and begins work when idle; **Done** updates conditions during work without resetting time or the work target. New work periods start without conditions. Selected conditions appear beneath the timer controls and can also be edited in recording history.

Conditions describe the whole recording segment, rather than timed changes. Saved names are snapshots: renaming or removing reusable choices does not rewrite history. Conditions remain in local recording metadata and exports, and are included in eligible shared recording archives under the existing sharing choices.
